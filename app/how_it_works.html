The words on the screen

Every term the app uses, with a picture that is the definition.

Win probability

The chance a team wins from this exact moment. 50% is a toss-up; it slides toward 100% or 0% as the game gets decided.

Pre-snap

Only what's knowable before the ball moves: score, clock, down, field position, the betting line. Never what the play did — that would be cheating.

Brier score

The model's report card. The average squared miss between its odds and what happened. 0 is perfect, 0.25 is a coin flip. Ours: 0.151.

Calibration

Honesty. When the model says 70%, do those teams really win about 70% of the time? Plotted, honesty is a straight diagonal line.

WP swing

How wild a game was. Add up every jump in the win-probability line. A comeback racks up a huge total; a wire-to-wire win barely moves.

Home margin

The final score gap for the home team. +7 means the home team won by 7; −3 means it lost by 3.

Vegas pregame

What the betting market expected before kickoff, turned into a win probability. The amber line on the Game chart — a yardstick for the model.

What actually happened

The game ends. There's no 72% — the home team either won (100%) or lost (0%). Say they won. The gap between our 72% and reality is 0.28.

3

Score it: Brier

Square the gap so big misses hurt more: 0.28² = 0.078. Do that for every play in every game and average. A coin-flip guesser lands at 0.25; PreSnap scores 0.151.

4

Are the odds honest?

Round up every play where the model said about 70%. If it's honest, roughly 70% of those teams actually won. Plot predicted vs. actual — landing on the diagonal means the odds are trustworthy.

5

How wild was it?

Follow the win-probability line for a whole game and add up every jump. A calm game barely stirs; a back-and-forth thriller piles up a big WP swing — that's how the Rankings tab finds the season's chaos.
